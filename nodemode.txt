Got it 👍 You’re essentially describing a utility app for managing node_modules (or other heavy folders) with a lightweight tray interface. You’re right that the challenge is how to split free vs. paid features so the app feels useful right away, but also has a natural upgrade path.

Here’s a structured suggestion:

⸻

🔹 General Rule for Free vs Paid Split
	•	Free version → should solve the core pain (big node_modules eating space), but in a basic/manual way.
	•	Paid version (Pro) → should provide convenience, automation, insights, and peace of mind.

⸻

✅ Free (Core Functions)
	1.	Manual scan & cleanup
	•	Scan a selected folder/project.
	•	Show list of node_modules (with size).
	•	Allow manual removal.
(this is the minimum that makes the app immediately useful)
	2.	Basic stats
	•	Show how much space was freed after cleanup.
	•	Maybe a history of last X cleanups (local only).
	3.	Simple UI in tray
	•	“Scan now” button.
	•	Quick access to most recent projects.

⸻

💎 Pro (Paid Features)
	1.	Automation
	•	Scheduled scans (daily/weekly).
	•	Auto-clean based on rules (e.g., remove node_modules older than N days).
	2.	Global scanning / background watcher
	•	Automatically discover new node_modules across the disk.
	•	Option to exclude/include directories.
	3.	Advanced stats & insights
	•	Space saved over time (with charts).
	•	Which projects eat the most space.
	•	Trend over weeks/months.
	4.	Smart rules
	•	Delete only if project hasn’t been opened for N days.
	•	Keep only the latest X folders per workspace.
	•	Exclude specific projects.
	5.	Extras (can come later based on feedback):
	•	Notifications (“You have 20 GB in unused node_modules”).
	•	Export/share stats.
	•	Multi-platform sync (if you ever go beyond macOS).

⸻

💡 Why this split works
	•	Free users immediately solve their main problem (clear space).
	•	Paid users pay for not thinking about it anymore (automation, rules, stats).
	•	You keep the entry barrier low but have a clear “unlock convenience” upsell.

⸻

🔮 Strategy for v1
	•	Start tiny: free app with manual scan/cleanup + size stats.
	•	Add Pro features only after you see adoption.
	•	Use feedback to refine (maybe users want notifications more than charts, etc.).

⸻

👉 Would you like me to also suggest a minimal UX flow for the tray app (how the menu could look with free vs pro items)? That could help you visualize how the split feels to a user.

1. Set Up Your Patreon Tier or Digital Product
Create a membership tier or one-time digital product on Patreon that offers the app/license key as a reward.

Clearly state in the tier/product description that patrons will receive a license key after supporting or purchasing.

2. Store License Keys
Prepare a list of license keys (in a Google Sheet, Airtable, or similar database) that you want to deliver to your buyers or patrons.

3. Automate Key Delivery (Recommended)
You can use third-party automation services like Zapier, Appy Pie Automate, or Albato to handle this process:

Connect Patreon to Automation Service: Link your Patreon account to Zapier or Appy Pie by following their authentication steps.

Trigger: Set up a trigger for a new member, new pledge, or product purchase event on your Patreon campaign.

Action: When the trigger fires, use the automation tool to:

Select an unused key from your database.

Send the license key to the patron via email or Patreon’s direct message.

Mark the key as “used” in your database to avoid duplicates.

4. Manual Delivery (If preferred)
For low volumes, you could manually send a unique license key to each patron via email or Patreon message after confirming their purchase.

Additional Tips
Make sure to attach a license agreement with your product to clarify usage rights.

If you want to bypass integrations, some creators use third-party platforms like Gumroad, Itch.io, or Shopify—these platforms can generate and distribute keys automatically but may not directly connect with Patreon.
